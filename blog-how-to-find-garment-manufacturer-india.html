How to Find a Garment Manufacturer in India

India is one of the world's largest garment exporters — but finding the right manufacturer takes more than a Google search. Here's exactly what to look for, what questions to ask, and how to avoid the most common mistakes.

By Underroot Fashion LLP · 8 min read

Why source from India?

India is the second-largest textile exporter in the world. The country has a complete ecosystem — from raw cotton and yarn to weaving, dyeing, cutting, stitching, and finishing — all within reach. For international brands, this means:

  • Lower manufacturing costs compared to Europe or the US
  • Strong fabric and raw material base (cotton, linen, blends)
  • Flexible MOQs — from small startup batches to large retail orders
  • English-speaking manufacturers and business culture
  • Established export infrastructure across key garment hubs

Key manufacturing hubs include Delhi NCR (shirts, uniforms), Tirupur (knitwear, T-shirts), Mumbai (fashion and exports), Surat (synthetic fabrics), and Ludhiana (woollens and knitwear).

Step 1 — Define what you need before you search

Before contacting any manufacturer, be clear on:

  • Product type — woven shirts, knitwear, uniforms, denim, activewear?
  • Fabric — cotton, polyester, blends? Do you have a fabric preference or will you let the manufacturer source?
  • MOQ — how many pieces are you ordering per style per colour? Be realistic.
  • Branding requirements — woven label, hang tags, packaging, embroidery?
  • Timeline — when do you need delivery?
  • Budget — what's your target FOB cost per piece?

A manufacturer needs all of this to give you a serious quote. Vague enquiries get vague responses — or no response at all.

Step 2 — Where to find manufacturers

There are several ways to find Indian garment manufacturers:

Trade directories

IndiaMart, TradeIndia, and Alibaba list thousands of Indian manufacturers. The challenge is filtering genuine factories from traders and middlemen. Always ask: "Do you own the factory or do you outsource production?"

Trade shows

Apparel Export Promotion Council (AEPC) organises India ITME and other textile expos. Meeting manufacturers in person at a trade show gives you a much better read on them than email.

Google search

Search for specific product + location, like "woven shirt manufacturer Delhi" or "uniform manufacturer Noida". Manufacturers who invest in their website are usually more serious about exports.

LinkedIn

Reach out directly to founders, export managers, or merchandising heads on LinkedIn. A direct message often gets a faster and more genuine response than a contact form.

Referrals

The most reliable method. Ask other brands in your category who they use. A warm referral saves months of trial and error.

Step 3 — Questions to ask before committing

Once you have a shortlist, ask every manufacturer these questions:

Factory & capacity

  • Do you own your factory or outsource to other units?
  • What is your monthly production capacity?
  • How many stitching machines do you have?
  • Can I visit your factory before placing an order?

Product & quality

  • Have you made this product category before? Can I see samples?
  • What is your quality check process?
  • What is your defect rate and how do you handle rejections?
  • Do you have an in-house design team or do I need to send tech packs?

Commercial

  • What is your minimum order quantity per style?
  • What are your payment terms?
  • What is the typical turnaround time from order confirmation to dispatch?
  • Do you handle export documentation (invoice, packing list, GST/IGST)?

Step 4 — Red flags to watch for

  • No factory address or photos — legitimate manufacturers are not shy about showing their facility.
  • Unrealistically low pricing — quality garments have a real cost. If someone quotes 40% below market, ask why.
  • No samples before bulk order — any serious manufacturer will do a sample run. Never go straight to bulk.
  • Vague communication on quality process — if they can't explain their QC steps, they probably don't have a structured one.
  • Middlemen posing as factories — always confirm whether you're speaking to the actual production unit or an agent who will outsource your order.
  • No track record with similar buyers — ask for client references, especially brands in your size and category.

Step 5 — Start with a sample order

Never place a large bulk order with a new manufacturer without a sample run first. A sample order (typically 1–3 pieces per style) lets you assess:

  • Stitch quality and finishing
  • Fabric hand feel and colour accuracy
  • Construction — seams, buttons, interlining
  • Measurement accuracy against your spec sheet
  • Communication quality and turnaround time

A good manufacturer will treat your sample as seriously as a bulk order. If the sample process is sloppy, the bulk order will be worse.

What makes a good manufacturing partner

Beyond quality, the best manufacturers offer:

  • Fabric backing — manufacturers connected to fabric mills can control quality and cost better than those who buy from the open market.
  • In-house design support — if you're a new brand without a tech pack, an experienced design team can help you develop your product from scratch.
  • Transparent communication — quick response times, clear production updates, and honest conversation about problems.
  • Scalability — a manufacturer who can handle your first 500-piece order should also be able to handle 50,000 as you grow.
