What to Look for in a Uniform Supplier

Uniforms represent your brand every single day. The wrong supplier means inconsistent quality, missed deadlines, and staff wearing something you're not proud of. Here's how to choose the right one.

By Underroot Fashion LLP · 6 min read

Why uniform procurement is different from regular clothing

Buying uniforms for your organisation is not like buying off-the-shelf clothing. You need:

  • Consistent colour and fabric across potentially hundreds of pieces
  • Specific size runs to fit a diverse workforce
  • Durability — uniforms get washed and worn daily
  • Custom branding — your logo, your colours, your design
  • Repeat orders that match your original batch exactly
  • Reliable delivery — staff can't go without uniforms

A supplier who is great for a one-off fashion order may completely fail on a uniform order. These are different capabilities.

1. Manufacturing capability — do they actually make uniforms?

Many suppliers will say yes to anything to win your business. Dig deeper:

  • Ask for photos of past uniform orders they have delivered
  • Ask for client references in your sector — a hotel uniform order is different from a school uniform order
  • Ask if they have done orders of your scale before
  • Confirm they own and operate the production facility (not a trader outsourcing to unknown units)

2. Fabric quality and consistency

Fabric is the foundation of a good uniform. Key things to check:

  • Colour fastness — does the colour hold after repeated washing? Corporate uniforms in particular must not fade unevenly across a team.
  • Shrinkage control — fabric that hasn't been pre-shrunk will cause fitting problems after the first wash.
  • Batch consistency — fabric from different lots can vary in shade. A good supplier controls this carefully.
  • Appropriate weight — fabric gsm should match the use case. A security uniform needs heavier fabric than a hospitality front-desk shirt.

Suppliers with their own fabric backing (through a sister mill or in-house sourcing) have much better control over this than those buying from the open market.

3. Customisation capability

Your uniform needs to carry your brand. Check that the supplier can handle:

Logo application

  • Embroidery (most professional, most durable)
  • Screen printing
  • Heat transfer / sublimation
  • Woven badges

Branding details

  • Custom woven neck labels
  • Brand-coloured buttons
  • Custom piping and trims
  • Specific Pantone colour matching

4. Size range and fit

A diverse workforce needs a proper size range. Your supplier should be able to produce:

  • Standard sizes (S to 3XL at minimum)
  • Custom sizes for specific employees if required
  • Separate patterns for men and women where applicable
  • Accurate measurements — consistent across the full size run

Ask for a size specification sheet before production. Confirm every measurement. A poorly sized uniform is uncomfortable, unprofessional, and will generate complaints from day one.

5. Quality control process

Uniform orders often have tight deadlines — there's no time to reject and redo an entire batch after delivery. Your supplier needs a structured quality check process, not just a final inspection.

A good QC process checks at multiple stages:

  1. Fabric inspection before cutting — checks for defects, shade variation, shrinkage
  2. Cut piece check — verifies panels are cut to spec before stitching begins
  3. In-line check — random checks during stitching to catch issues early
  4. End-of-line check — each finished garment checked before finishing
  5. Final packing check — quantity, size, label, packaging verified before dispatch

Ask your shortlisted suppliers to explain their QC process. If they can't describe it clearly, they probably don't have a formal one.

6. Delivery reliability

Late uniform delivery is not a minor inconvenience — it can disrupt operations, create HR issues, and embarrass your organisation. When evaluating a supplier:

  • Ask about their on-time delivery track record
  • Confirm their production lead time in writing before placing the order
  • Ask how they handle delays — do they communicate proactively?
  • Check if they can handle repeat/top-up orders quickly (staff turnover is a reality)

7. Repeat order consistency

This is where many uniform suppliers fail. Your second order must match your first — in colour, fabric, fit, and finish. A new employee's uniform must look identical to a uniform ordered two years earlier.

Ask your supplier: "How do you maintain consistency across repeat orders?" A good answer includes fabric lot records, approved fabric swatches kept on file, and a clear reference for every specification agreed in the original order.

Quick checklist before you sign a uniform order

  • ✓Visited or verified the manufacturing facility
  • ✓Reviewed samples in your actual fabric and colour
  • ✓Confirmed full size run availability
  • ✓Tested fabric for colour fastness and shrinkage
  • ✓Verified logo/embroidery capability with a sample
  • ✓Confirmed lead time in writing
  • ✓Understood repeat order process and timeline
  • ✓Checked payment terms and cancellation policy
  • ✓Spoken to at least one reference client
