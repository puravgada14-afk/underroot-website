What to Look for in a Uniform Supplier

Uniforms represent your brand every single day. The wrong supplier means inconsistent quality, missed deadlines, and staff wearing something you're not proud of. Here's what to check before you sign any uniform contract.

By Underroot Fashion LLP·7 min read

Why uniform procurement is different

Buying uniforms for your school, hotel, hospital, or corporate team is not like buying off-the-shelf clothing. You need:

  • Consistent colour and fabric across hundreds — sometimes thousands — of pieces
  • Specific size runs to fit a diverse workforce
  • Durability — uniforms are washed and worn daily
  • Custom branding — your logo, your colours, your design
  • Repeat orders that match your original batch exactly
  • Reliable delivery — operations depend on it

A supplier great for a one-off fashion order may completely fail on a bulk uniform order. These are different capabilities — and you need to check for them specifically.

1. Do they actually manufacture uniforms?

Many suppliers say yes to anything to win your business. Verify:

  • Ask for photos or samples of past uniform orders in your sector
  • Ask for client references — a school uniform order is different from a hospitality order
  • Confirm they own and operate the production facility — not a trader outsourcing to unknown units
  • Ask how many uniform orders they have completed at your scale

2. Fabric quality and batch consistency

Fabric is the foundation of a good uniform. Check for:

  • Colour fastness — the colour must hold after repeated washing. Corporate uniforms especially must not fade unevenly across a team.
  • Shrinkage control — fabric that isn't pre-shrunk will cause fitting problems after the first wash.
  • Batch consistency — fabric from different production lots can vary in shade. A good uniform supplier controls this carefully.
  • Appropriate weight — fabric GSM should match the use case. A security uniform needs heavier fabric than a front-desk hospitality shirt.

Suppliers with their own fabric backing — through a sister mill or integrated sourcing — have far better control over this than those buying from the open market.

3. Customisation capability

Your uniform must carry your brand. Confirm the supplier can handle:

Logo application

  • Embroidery — most professional, most durable
  • Screen printing
  • Heat transfer / sublimation
  • Woven badges

Brand details

  • Custom woven neck labels
  • Brand-coloured buttons and piping
  • Pantone colour matching
  • Custom packaging per branch

4. Size range and fit accuracy

A diverse workforce needs a proper size range. Your supplier should be able to produce:

  • Full size range — S to 3XL at minimum, with custom sizes available if needed
  • Separate patterns for men and women where applicable
  • Accurate measurements — consistent across the full size run, not just one size

Ask for a size specification sheet before production. Confirm every measurement. A poorly fitted uniform is uncomfortable, unprofessional, and generates complaints from day one.

5. Quality control process

Uniform orders often have tight deadlines — there's no time to reject and redo an entire batch after delivery. Your supplier needs a structured quality check process, not just a final inspection.

A structured QC process checks at multiple stages:

  1. Fabric inspection — checks for defects, shade variation, shrinkage before cutting
  2. Cut piece check — verifies panels are cut to spec before stitching begins
  3. In-line check — random checks during stitching to catch issues early
  4. End-of-line check — each finished garment checked before finishing
  5. Final packing check — quantity, size, label, packaging verified before dispatch

Ask your shortlisted suppliers to explain their QC process specifically. If they can't describe it clearly, they probably don't have a formal one.

6. Delivery reliability and repeat orders

Late uniform delivery disrupts operations and creates HR problems. Check:

  • Ask for their on-time delivery track record and client references
  • Get the production lead time in writing before placing your order
  • Confirm how they handle top-up orders — staff turnover is a reality
  • Ask how they maintain consistency on repeat orders — fabric records, approved swatches, spec sheets on file

Checklist before signing a uniform order

  • ✓Visited or verified the manufacturing facility
  • ✓Reviewed samples in your actual fabric and colour
  • ✓Confirmed full size run availability
  • ✓Tested fabric for colour fastness and shrinkage
  • ✓Verified logo embroidery / print with a sample piece
  • ✓Confirmed lead time in writing
  • ✓Understood repeat order process
  • ✓Checked payment terms and cancellation policy
  • ✓Spoken to at least one reference client in your sector

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum order for corporate uniforms in India?

MOQ varies by manufacturer, garment type, and customisation requirements. Always confirm the exact minimum directly with the supplier before shortlisting them.

How do I ensure uniform quality across multiple branches or locations?

Choose a supplier who maintains approved fabric swatches, colour standards, and spec sheets on file. Every repeat order should be checked against the approved original. Ask specifically how they manage this before placing your first order.

What fabrics work best for hotel or hospitality uniforms?

Breathable, easy-care fabrics are ideal for hospitality uniforms — cotton-polyester blends, poplin, and stretch fabrics work well depending on the role. Front-of-house staff benefit from lighter, presentable fabrics; housekeeping needs durability and ease of washing. Ask your manufacturer for fabric recommendations specific to each role.

How do I get my logo embroidered on uniforms?

Provide your logo in vector format (AI or EPS) to your manufacturer. They will digitise it for embroidery and produce a sample piece for your approval before production begins. Always approve the embroidery sample before bulk production.

Can a uniform manufacturer in India export to Australia or the UK?

Yes. Indian garment manufacturers regularly export to Australia, UK, Middle East, and other markets. For Australian buyers, the India-Australia ECTA (Economic Cooperation and Trade Agreement) offers preferential duty rates on garment imports from India. Check with your customs broker for the exact applicable rates for your product category.
