How to Start a Clothing Brand with Low MOQ

High minimum order quantities stop most clothing brand ideas before they start. But the right manufacturer can get you to market with a few hundred pieces — and scale from there. Here's how to do it right.

By Underroot Fashion LLP · 7 min read

What is MOQ and why does it matter?

MOQ stands for Minimum Order Quantity — the smallest number of pieces a manufacturer will produce in a single order. For most large Indian factories, MOQ starts at 1,000–3,000 pieces per style. For new brands, that's a lot of capital tied up in inventory before you've made a single sale.

Low MOQ manufacturing (typically 100–500 pieces per style) lets you test the market, validate your designs, and build your brand without betting everything on a single bulk order.

Step 1 — Start with a focused product line

The most common mistake new clothing brands make is trying to launch too many products at once. More styles mean more MOQs, more capital, more complexity, and more risk.

Instead, start with 2–4 core styles in 2–3 colours. This keeps your MOQ per style manageable and lets you put real energy into getting those products exactly right.

For example: a men's shirt brand could launch with one Oxford shirt and one poplin shirt — two styles, done exceptionally well — rather than a full 12-piece collection done averagely.

Step 2 — Create a tech pack (or find someone who can help)

A tech pack is a document that tells a manufacturer exactly how to make your garment. It includes:

  • Technical sketch (front, back, side views)
  • Measurements for each size
  • Fabric specifications (weight, composition, weave)
  • Trim details (buttons, thread colour, zippers, labels)
  • Stitching specifications (stitch type, SPI — stitches per inch)
  • Branding placement (label, hang tag, embroidery)

If you don't have a tech pack, that's fine — look for manufacturers who have an in-house design and merchandising team. They can help you develop the tech pack from a reference sample or even a rough sketch. This is a major advantage of working with a full-service manufacturer.

Step 3 — Find the right manufacturer for your volume

Not every manufacturer accepts low MOQ orders. Large export houses focused on international retail accounts won't be interested in 200-piece orders. You need to find manufacturers who:

  • Explicitly work with startups and new brands
  • Have flexible MOQ policies (not just a marketing claim — ask specifically)
  • Offer design support so you don't need to arrive with a complete tech pack
  • Have experience with private label and custom branding

Delhi NCR and Noida-based manufacturers are often more flexible on MOQ than Tirupur or Surat facilities, which are geared toward large export volumes.

Step 4 — Always start with a sample

Before any bulk order, request a sample — ideally a pre-production sample made in your actual fabric and trims. A sample typically costs ₹500–₹2,000 per piece depending on the garment complexity.

When you receive the sample, check:

  • Measurements against your size chart
  • Fabric hand feel and colour vs. your approval swatch
  • Stitch quality — even, no skipped stitches, clean finishing
  • Construction details — collar, cuffs, placket, buttons
  • Label and branding placement

Send back clear, written feedback — not just "it doesn't look right." The more specific your comments, the better the revision will be.

Per-piece cost is higher on low MOQ orders because setup costs (cutting, pattern making) are spread over fewer pieces. As your volumes increase, the per-piece cost drops significantly.

Step 6 — Think about branding from day one

Private label manufacturing means your brand name goes on the garment — not the manufacturer's. Make sure your manufacturer offers:

  • Custom woven labels (neck label, care label, size label)
  • Hang tags with your branding
  • Embroidery or print for logo placement
  • Custom packaging (polybag printing, tissue paper, boxes)

Even on a small first order, branded packaging makes your product feel premium and builds customer trust. It's worth the small extra cost.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing manufacturer based on price alone — the cheapest quote usually means cut corners somewhere. Compare total value, not just price per piece.
  • Skipping the sample stage — it costs a fraction of a bulk order and saves enormous headaches.
  • Launching too many styles — depth over breadth, especially at the start.
  • No written agreement — always confirm order specifications, price, timeline, and payment terms in writing.
  • Unrealistic timelines — a proper production run typically takes 3–6 weeks from order confirmation. Factor this into your launch planning.
